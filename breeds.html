Breed Longevity Database

How long do dogs really live? Start with the breed.

Typical lifespan ranges for America’s most common breeds. Search, filter by size, and notice the pattern: the bigger the dog, the shorter the average life — which is exactly why large-breed owners have the most to gain from the new longevity science.

Famously hardy. Their biggest longevity risk is lifestyle — they stay high-energy well into old age.

Long-lived and spirited. Trachea and dental care deserve early attention.

Regularly reaches the mid-teens. Dental cleanings make an outsized difference in Yorkies.

Heart screening is everything: mitral valve disease is the breed’s main lifespan limiter. Ask your vet about cardiac checks from age 5.

Shih TzuSmall · 9–16 lb · senior from ~9
10–16 yrstypical lifespan

Bred purely for companionship for a thousand years — and built to last. Eyes and breathing need care in heat.

French BulldogSmall · under 28 lb · senior from ~8
10–12 yrstypical lifespan

America’s most popular breed. Breathing, spine and heat sensitivity cap the lifespan — keep them lean and cool.

BeagleMedium · 20–25 lb · senior from ~9
12–15 yrstypical lifespan

Sturdy and long-lived — if you win the battle with the food drive. Beagles gain weight by looking at a kitchen.

Border CollieMedium · 35–45 lb · senior from ~9
12–15 yrstypical lifespan

Athletes into double digits. Joint care and a job to do keep body and brain young.

Australian ShepherdMedium · 50–65 lb · senior from ~9
12–15 yrstypical lifespan

Hardy working breed. Ask breeders about MDR1 gene testing — it changes which medications are safe.

Siberian HuskyMedium · 35–60 lb · senior from ~9
12–14 yrstypical lifespan

Remarkably free of genetic disease for a purebred. Weight creeps up when the running years slow.

Mixed Breed (medium)Medium · varies · senior from ~9

Mixed-breed dogs often outlive similar-sized purebreds thanks to genetic diversity — though size still sets the range.

Cocker SpanielMedium · 25–30 lb · senior from ~9
10–14 yrstypical lifespan

Ears and weight are the management priorities; both are very fixable with routine care.

English BulldogMedium · 40–50 lb · senior from ~7
8–10 yrstypical lifespan

Breathing anatomy limits everything. Weight, heat and anesthesia planning matter more here than almost anywhere.

Labrador RetrieverLarge · 55–80 lb · senior from ~8
10–12 yrstypical lifespan

The Purina study breed: lean Labs gained a median 1.8 years. Some Labs carry a gene variant that makes them perpetually hungry — measure everything.

Golden RetrieverLarge · 55–75 lb · senior from ~8
10–12 yrstypical lifespan

Cancer is the breed’s leading cause of death — lifetime-study data puts it near 60%. New-lump checks and early screening matter enormously.

BoxerLarge · 50–80 lb · senior from ~8
10–12 yrstypical lifespan

Cancer and heart rhythm issues are the watch-outs. Any fainting episode in a Boxer is a vet visit, not a wait-and-see.

German ShepherdLarge · 50–90 lb · senior from ~8
9–13 yrstypical lifespan

Hips and mobility define their later years. Muscle maintenance and pain honesty extend the good years.

RottweilerLarge · 80–135 lb · senior from ~7
8–10 yrstypical lifespan

Bone cancer risk is the shadow over this breed. Persistent limping in a Rottweiler is never ‘just a sprain’ until a vet says so.

Great DaneGiant · 110–175 lb · senior from ~6
7–10 yrstypical lifespan

The shortest-lived common breed — and a prime target for IGF-1 drugs like LOY-001. Bloat is the emergency every Dane owner must know.

Beloved and heartbreakingly brief, largely due to cancer. Swiss breeders’ longevity programs are slowly moving the number.

Ranges are the typical published figures from breed references (AKC / PetMD) and veterinary sources, rounded to whole years. They describe populations, not your dog: weight, dental health, genetics and luck move an individual dog years in either direction. “Senior begins” is roughly the last quarter of the typical lifespan — when twice-yearly vet checks should start.
