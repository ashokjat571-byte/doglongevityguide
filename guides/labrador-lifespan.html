How Long Do Labradors Live? Lifespan, Health Risks and the Lean Lesson

The Labrador Retriever has been America's most popular dog for decades, and its lifespan is well documented: typically 10 to 12 years, with well-cared-for Labs commonly reaching 12–13. The Labrador also happens to be the breed that taught science its most important longevity lesson.

The short version: Expect 10–12 years. Labs are prone to obesity, hip and elbow dysplasia, and exercise-induced collapse of their health in old age usually traces back to weight and joints. The landmark 14-year lifetime study was done on Labradors themselves: dogs kept lean lived a median of 13.0 years versus 11.2 — nearly two extra years from portion control alone.

The breed's main health risks

  • Obesity — Labs carry a famous appetite, and a gene variant (POMC) found in many Labradors makes them unusually food-motivated. Weight is not a cosmetic issue in this breed; it drives joint disease, diabetes and shorter life.
  • Hip and elbow dysplasia — common, painful and worsened by excess weight and over-exercise in puppyhood.
  • Cancer — including lymphoma and mast cell tumours; check every lump.
  • Ears and eyes — floppy ears trap moisture (regular checks prevent chronic infections); inherited eye conditions are screened in good breeding lines.

What gives a Lab the best odds

  • Weigh food, weigh the dog, monthly. In this breed, "he looks fine" is not a measurement. Target body condition 4–5 out of 9 — ribs felt easily, waist visible.
  • Exercise daily, protect joints while growing: no forced long runs or repetitive stairs for puppies; swimming is the classic Lab-friendly, joint-friendly exercise at every age.
  • Senior screening from age 8, twice yearly, with bloodwork.
  • Feed the senior in front of you — generous quality protein for muscle, controlled calories, omega-3s for joints and brain (see our senior food guide).

If any breed proves that longevity is mostly management, it is this one: the genetics give a decade, and the owner's measuring cup decides how much more.
