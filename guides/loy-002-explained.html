LOY-002 Explained: The Dog Longevity Pill That Cleared Two FDA Milestones

If you have seen headlines about a pill that could help dogs live longer, they were almost certainly about LOY-002, made by the San Francisco biotech company Loyal. It is the most advanced dog longevity drug in the world — and the most misunderstood. Here is what is actually known.

The short version: LOY-002 is a daily prescription pill being developed for dogs aged 10 and older, weighing at least 14 pounds. The US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) has accepted that it has a "reasonable expectation of effectiveness" (February 2025) and has accepted its safety data (January 2026). It is not yet approved or on sale, and it has not yet been proven to extend any dog's life.

What is LOY-002 supposed to do?

Unlike a drug that treats one disease, LOY-002 targets the biology of aging itself. As dogs age, their metabolism changes — insulin handling worsens and age-related diseases become more likely. LOY-002 is designed to improve metabolic health in senior dogs, with the goal of delaying age-related disease and preserving vitality: more healthy years, not just more years.

What has the FDA actually accepted?

Two milestones matter. First, in February 2025, the FDA's Center for Veterinary Medicine accepted LOY-002's reasonable expectation of effectiveness — a key step on the FDA's expanded conditional approval pathway for innovative animal drugs. Second, Loyal's safety package was accepted in January 2026. Approval is not guaranteed, and a launch date has not been confirmed; reporting has pointed to a possible 2026 arrival, but treat any date as provisional until the FDA grants conditional approval.

What is the STAY study?

The real proof will come from Loyal's STAY study — a large clinical trial that has enrolled more than 1,300 senior dogs across around 70 veterinary practices in the United States. Dogs in the study are followed over years to see whether LOY-002 dogs stay healthier for longer than dogs on placebo. Until those results are published, every claim about LOY-002 "adding years" is a hope, not a finding.

LOY-002 vs LOY-001 and LOY-003

Loyal's other two candidates target a different pathway: IGF-1, a growth hormone that is much higher in large dogs and is linked to their shorter lifespans. LOY-001 is a long-acting injection and LOY-003 a daily pill, both aimed at large and giant breeds. The FDA has accepted the effectiveness section for LOY-003 as well.

What should dog parents do now?

  • Do not buy "longevity pills" sold online today claiming to be LOY-002 — the real drug is not on the market.
  • Ask your veterinarian to note your interest, so you hear when conditional approval and prescribing begin.
  • Use the waiting time well: weight, exercise, dental care and senior bloodwork are proven today, and they are free or cheap.
