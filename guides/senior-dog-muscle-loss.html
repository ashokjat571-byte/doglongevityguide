Muscle Loss in Old Dogs: Why It Happens and How to Slow It

Run your hands over an old dog's hips and spine. If the bones feel sharper than they used to and the thigh muscles look thinner, you are feeling sarcopenia — age-related muscle loss. It is one of the most important and most overlooked processes in canine aging, because muscle is what keeps an old dog walking, warm, balanced and independent.

The short version: Muscle loss in seniors is driven by aging itself, lower protein use, inactivity and often hidden pain. The countermeasures with evidence: adequate high-quality protein (healthy seniors need more, not less), daily gentle activity, small strength-building exercises, and a vet check to rule out pain and disease. Sudden or rapid loss is a warning sign, not normal aging.

Why old dogs lose muscle

  • Anabolic resistance: aging muscle responds less to dietary protein, so the same food builds less muscle than it did at age 4.
  • Doing less: stiff, sore dogs move less; unused muscle disappears fast — a spiral where pain causes loss and loss worsens pain.
  • Disease: kidney disease, cancer, heart disease and hormonal problems can all consume muscle. One-sided or rapid loss, or loss with weight drop, deserves prompt veterinary investigation.

Protein: feed the muscle you want to keep

Unless your vet has prescribed a therapeutic diet (for example for advanced kidney disease), healthy seniors do best on diets with generous, high-quality animal protein. Check muscle condition, not just the scale: weight can stay stable while muscle is quietly replaced by fat — the pattern vets call "fat but frail."

Gentle strength work that helps

  • Short, frequent walks — three 10–15 minute walks beat one long one.
  • Sit-to-stand repetitions: ask for 3–5 slow sits with good form, a few times a day — dog squats, kindly disguised as training.
  • Gentle hills and varied surfaces build legs and balance; swimming or hydrotherapy unloads sore joints while loading muscle.
  • Control pain first. A dog who hurts will not exercise. Modern arthritis care is the unlock for everything above — see our joint care guide.

Muscle is a senior dog's savings account. Protein is the deposit, movement is what stops the withdrawals, and your vet is the auditor who makes sure nothing is stealing from it.
