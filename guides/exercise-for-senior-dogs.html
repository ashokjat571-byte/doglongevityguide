Exercise for Senior Dogs: How Much Is Right, and What Kind?

The most common mistake in senior dog care is kindness misdirected: the dog slows down, so the owner stops walking him — and the muscle, joint health and mental sharpness that only movement maintains quietly drain away. Senior dogs do not need less exercise. They need different exercise.

The short version: Most healthy senior dogs do well with 30–60 total minutes a day, split into 2–4 short sessions, at a pace where they finish comfortable, not exhausted. Favour walking with sniffing, swimming and gentle hills; retire the weekend-warrior pattern. In Dog Aging Project data, dogs active an hour or more daily had dramatically lower odds of cognitive decline.

The dose that works

  • Little and often. Three 15-minute walks protect joints and energy far better than one 45-minute march.
  • The next-day test. Judge a session by the following morning: a dog who is stiffer or reluctant the next day did too much yesterday. Cut by a third and rebuild slowly.
  • Warm up, end easy. Five gentle minutes at the start and end; no sprinting out of the car into cold muscles.
  • Consistency beats intensity. Daily rhythm also anchors sleep and digestion — and protects against the nighttime restlessness of cognitive decline.

Best kinds of senior exercise

  • Sniff walks — slow, nose-led exploration; high mental value, low joint cost.
  • Swimming / hydrotherapy — the gold standard for arthritic dogs: full-body muscle work with no impact.
  • Gentle hills and varied ground — build strength and balance in small doses.
  • Home strength games — sit-to-stand sets, slow figure-eights around cones, standing balance on a cushion (with your vet's or physio's guidance).

Red flags — stop and talk to your vet

  • Lagging behind on walks the dog used to enjoy, sitting down mid-walk, or refusing to go.
  • Panting that doesn't settle, coughing, or pale/bluish gums during activity.
  • New limping lasting more than a day, or stiffness that is getting worse week by week.

Movement is medicine for an old dog — the art is in the dose. Small, daily and enjoyable beats heroic, rare and punishing, every single time.
