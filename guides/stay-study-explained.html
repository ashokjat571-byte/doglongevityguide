The STAY Study: 1,300 Dogs Are Testing Whether We Can Slow Canine Aging

Every claim about dog longevity drugs eventually runs into one question: where is the proof? The STAY study is where that proof is being built. Run by biotech company Loyal, it is the pivotal clinical trial of LOY-002 — and one of the largest studies of aging ever conducted in pet dogs.

The short version: The STAY study has enrolled more than 1,300 dogs aged 10 and older across around 70 veterinary practices in the United States. Half receive the daily LOY-002 pill and half a placebo, and researchers track their health over years to see whether the treated dogs stay healthier for longer. Results take time — aging cannot be hurried.

Why this study matters

Until now, no drug has ever been developed with the explicit goal of extending healthy lifespan in dogs, so no trial like this has existed. Regulators at the FDA accepted LOY-002's "reasonable expectation of effectiveness" in February 2025 based on earlier data — but the STAY study is the multi-year, real-world test in ordinary pet dogs, living at home with their families, that will show whether the effect is real and meaningful.

Who is in the study?

Participants are senior pet dogs — 10 years and older, weighing at least 14 pounds — examined at participating veterinary clinics across the US. They receive regular health assessments so researchers can compare not just how long dogs live, but their quality of life: mobility, cognition, and the onset of age-related disease.

What the study can and cannot tell us

  • It can show whether LOY-002 dogs, on average, stay healthier longer than placebo dogs.
  • It cannot promise results for any individual dog, and it will not read out overnight — lifespan studies are measured in years.
  • A positive result would be a landmark for veterinary medicine — and a proof of concept that aging itself can be treated, with implications researchers are watching far beyond dogs.

What owners should do meanwhile

Enrollment runs through participating clinics, but for most owners the practical move is simpler: follow the results, ask your vet about the program at senior visits, and use the proven basics today — lean weight, daily movement, dental care and twice-yearly senior screening.
